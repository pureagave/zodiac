# Tablet mounting — hardware & method

How the fleet tablets get mounted to a heavily-vibrating art car (stiff
suspension, lots of shake). Decided 2026-08-19. **No 3D-printed cradles** — all
off-the-shelf, orderable now.

## The system: RAM Mounts

Purpose-built for marine/motorcycle/off-road vibration. Modular ball-and-socket
in three parts that snap together — **the rubber ball-and-socket joint *is* the
anti-vibration**, which is the whole reason to use RAM over a rigid arm.

1. **Cradle** (holds the tablet) — RAM **X-Grip**, spring-loaded, width-adjustable, no per-device fitting.
2. **Arm** (the damper) — RAM **double-socket arm** between two rubber balls. Ball size **B (1")** for phones/10" tablets, **C (1.5")** for the heavy S9+.
3. **Base** (screws to the car) — RAM **diamond/round drill-down base**.

## Method notes (the parts that actually fail)

- **Fiberglass cracks under point loads + vibration.** Spread the load with fender
  washers/backing plate — never a bare screw head on gelcoat. Prefer **well nuts**.
  **Blue Loctite on every threaded joint** + lock washers. Bed the base in a thin
  layer of silicone/butyl (extra damping + seal). Don't over-torque.
- **Metal:** self-tappers or rivnuts/nutserts + threadlocker.
- **Cable strain relief is not optional.** Vibration works the **USB/power
  connector loose** long before the mount fails — right-angle plugs, clamp the
  cable to the arm within a few inches of the connector, leave a service loop. A
  tablet that stays mounted but drops power at 2am is a failed mount.
- **The beacon** (XCover) is headless but critical (fleet GPS) — VHB it to a flat
  spot so it can't rattle loose; it doesn't need a display mount.

## Avoid

Magnetic mounts (shift/detach under vibration), suction cups (fail with heat +
shake), all-rigid-plastic arms (fatigue-crack). The rubber ball joint is the point.
