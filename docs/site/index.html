Galactic Relay · Black Rock City

One system in three parts

Zodiac is the software for a Judge-Dredd-themed mutant vehicle (art car). A fleet of Android tablets shows an 80s green-phosphor cockpit over a live Black Rock City map; a phone acts as the vehicle's shared sensor hub; and a roof-mounted vision box watches for people in the dark and tells every screen — and a spotlight — where they are.

Everything realtime runs on the vehicle's own WiFi. Starlink is aboard but is treated as unreliable; nothing safety-relevant depends on it.

How the three parts talk

The three codebases share no code. They agree through two documented wire formats — NMEA sentences and ZTHREAT frames — and both are pinned by tests. Those two formats are the spine of the whole system, which is why Protocols gets its own section here.

Both realtime streams are one-to-many multicast with a subnet-broadcast copy. Nothing unicasts, nothing handshakes, nothing has a hardcoded IP.

What is actually running

The single most useful thing to know about this system is where the seam between “built”, “built but unproven” and “not built” falls. It does not fall where a feature list would suggest.

Not built — every vehicle transport is fake

FakeTransportAdapter is the only implementation of TransportAdapter in the repository, and FakeTelemetryRepository the only production TelemetryRepository. The transport chips, CONNECT / DISCONNECT buttons, SetHeading / SetSpeed commands and the thermal / mode / link readouts they feed are scaffolding for a vehicle bus that does not exist yet.

Real vehicle data reaches the app only through NetworkLocationSource (NMEA) and NetworkThreatSource (ZTHREAT). The GPS location sources, by contrast, are real: there are working System, BLE, USB and Network implementations.

Known open bug — the two sides of the vehicle disagree about bearings

jetson/HARDWARE.md concludes, with a physics argument, that the Lepton Ultra Wide's quoted 160° field of view is the diagonal, giving ±64° horizontal, and the tablet agrees: SurroundRing.COVERED_ARCS is -64f..64f. But zvision's --fov-ref still defaults to h, and every documented example rig omits fovref=d, so the Python side computes bearings as if the camera covered ±80°.

The two sides currently disagree by up to 16° at the frame edge. Until the default flips or the tablet's covered arc widens, treat edge bearings as unverified. Full detail.

  • :app — the cockpit Built & verified All three concepts, playa map, routing, GPS sourcing including NET, burn-in, kiosk, logging, passenger display.
  • :beacon — the sensor hub Built & verified GPS and compass proven end to end; all seven sentence types under test. The five sensor channels have not yet been exercised in a moving vehicle.
  • jetson/ — the edge box Software built, no camera yet ZTHREAT bus proven with --source fake; DMX fixture characterised; Stream Deck driven on real hardware. No camera has been attached to the box.
  • Vehicle transports Not built BLE / USB / WiFi adapters are one fake implementation registered three times.

Data sources and licence

Black Rock City GIS — streets, plazas, blocks, trash fence, toilets and CPNs from burningmantech/innovate-GIS-data. The 2026 dataset is bundled in app/src/main/assets/brc/2026/; 2025 is kept alongside for reference. Used under the Innovate Terms of Service.

Art and camp locations — the Burning Man API (api.burningman.org), fetched offline-first and cached. Embargoed until roughly three weeks pre-event. The 2025 art layer was bundled from iBurnApp/iBurn-Data (MIT); the 2026 GIS ships no art layer at all.

The project is Apache 2.0. The bundled Black Rock City GIS data is not covered by that licence — it is used under the Innovate Terms of Service, and NOTICE in the repository carries the full third-party attribution.
