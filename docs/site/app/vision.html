Threats and the DRIVER HUD

The tablet consumes ZTHREAT frames from the Jetson and turns them into the one display in this system with a safety job. Every decision behind that display lives in core/vision/SurroundRing, with tests — not in the draw code.

Why demoEnabled has no default value

It once defaulted to true and production never overrode it, which put three fabricated contacts — one flagged as a collision course — on the driver's night HUD whenever the Jetson died. The default was removed so no call site can forget to decide; ZodiacApplication passes false, and the compiler enforces it. An empty-but-live feed is a real all-clear and must never trigger the demo fallback.

VisionFeed is surfaced, not hidden: the HUD status line reads DEMO explicitly, the surround ring's rim goes dotted, and the passenger “souls detected” card is dropped entirely unless the feed is LIVE. The word CLEAR may only appear when the feed is LIVE.

SurroundRing — all the decisions, none of the pixels

core/vision/SurroundRing holds bearing wrap, sector and band classification, angular clustering with hysteresis, the brake and check-rear rules, and the five-state status precedence. ui/concepts/SurroundRingCanvas draws it and nothing else.

Bands, not a radius

radiusFraction = 1 - size was rejected as misleading. Radius on a plan view is read metrically, and apparent thermal size cannot support that: a child at 5 m and an adult at 12 m have the same apparent size, someone prone or on a bike does not scale the same way, and apparent size goes as ~1/r so a linear mapping distorts closure rate into apparent acceleration.

BandsizeradiusFraction
FAR< 0.331.00
MID< 0.660.66
NEARelse0.34

Ordinal reads as “nearer than that one, roughly” and stops the metric reading cold. The 0.34 floor also clears the reticle arms (~0.15 R), which the old 0.18 floor did not. Radius is sensor prominence, not distance, and the KDoc says so.

Clustering

Thirty dots tell a driver nothing. Contacts sharing a Band within CLUSTER_DEG = 15f merge into one mark, so “crowd on the port bow” renders as one legible thing. MAX_BLIPS = 12 remains as a backstop.

  • Collision contacts are never merged — each stays individually drawn.
  • Mean bearing is computed circularly (mean of unit vectors), or a group straddling ±180 would average to dead ahead. That is a real case: a crowd behind the vehicle.
  • memberCount > 1 renders heavier, never as a printed number — text is head-down reading.

Hysteresis

blips() re-sorts every frame, so with noisy size estimates the cap boundary would flicker and marks would pop in and out — more attention-grabbing than any real signal. An incumbent must fall below a challenger by SWITCH_MARGIN = 0.08f before being displaced. Ad-hoc id = 0 contacts never latch: they have no stable identity. This mirrors tracker.py's switch_margin, which solves the same problem for the DMX head.

Speed-gated braking

People deliberately walk up to art cars

That is what art cars are for. Every one of them is a constant-bearing, looming track, so brakeAdvised would latch continuously while parked or boarding, and the driver would have fully tuned it out by the time it mattered at 8 km/h on open playa.

BRAKE_MIN_KPH = 5f. Below that the imperative goes quiet — contacts still draw, and collision blips still render red. Only the instruction is suppressed, because below a walking pace the driver can stop trivially.

brakeAdvised reads effectiveSpeedKph (gpsSpeedKph ?: speedKph). That is load-bearing: the gate previously read only the debug chips' speed, whose sole writer is the control-strip SPD chip, so on a real drive it was 0 forever and neither ! BRAKE ! nor the ! COLLISION COURSE ! banner could ever fire. The rear alert has no speed gate, which is exactly why the bench verification passed.

DriverAlerts latches both alerts through AlarmLatch (instant attack, 1.5 s release) so a one-frame contact cannot produce a one-frame warning.

Coverage arcs — the ring must not lie

The rig is one forward ultra-wide thermal, not a closed ring. zvision already computes blind sectors and ThreatProtocol puts none of it on the wire. A complete rim with an empty rear quadrant would read “nothing behind you” when the truth is “nothing watching behind you”.

// Bearings the sensor rig actually watches, as (from, to) inclusive in ±180.
// MUST be kept in step with the Jetson's --camera rig spec; there is no wire
// field for this yet, so this constant is the only thing keeping the ring honest.
val COVERED_ARCS: List<ClosedFloatingPointRange<Float>> = listOf(-64f..64f)

An uncovered sector must never render identically to a watched-and-clear one. Uncovered arcs draw as a dotted rim rather than a solid one.

Settled 2026-08-10 — both sides agree at ±64°

The tablet carries ±64°, and zvision's --fov-ref now defaults to d too, so the Python side computes the same ±64° by default — confirmed by measurement, not just the physics argument. This constant still has to be kept in step with the Jetson's --camera rig by hand, because there is no wire field for coverage. Full detail →

Status line and banner

Evaluated over all threats, not the forward-filtered list.

ConditionTextColour
visionFeed == ABSENTNO VISIONNightRed
brakeAdvised(all, speedKph)! BRAKE !   n CONTACTSNightRed
rearAlert(all)! CHECK REAR !   n CONTACTSNightRed
visionFeed == DEMOn CONTACTS   DEMONightGreen
elsen CONTACTS   CLEARNightGreen
  • The count must never disappear during an alert — losing it at the busiest moment is backwards, and it is what keeps clustering honest.
  • ! CHECK REAR !, not ! REAR !: an imperative matching ! BRAKE !. A bare noun reads as a fragment at 3am.
  • A simultaneous forward + rear collision shows ! BRAKE ! — braking is the more urgent instruction.
  • The centre banner is the primary alert channel because it is big and preattentive; the bottom-left status line is secondary. A rear collision gets the status line and no centre banner — a centre-screen flash for something astern is exactly the mistrust-training problem.

Z-order

Rear blips land near the bottom of the ring, which is exactly where near forward figures' feet are drawn. Resolved by paint order, not by moving things:

  1. Perspective grid
  2. Ring furniture (rim, ticks, coverage, wedge, hull)
  3. Non-collision blips
  4. Wireframe figures
  5. Collision blips — the only thing allowed to punch through a figure
  6. Reticle, text

A figure occluding its own blip is correct: the figure is the detail view for that contact. A collision mark is never occluded.

Night discipline

  • Static furniture is NightGrid (#00421E) only — never NightGreen.
  • No fills except the hull outline and collision blip interiors.
  • Nothing in white, yellow or amber. Palette stays green / red / purple.
  • The ring must barely raise the steady-state lit area; blips carry the signal.
  • Collision blips get a radial spoke drawn outward, so a closing contact is findable by shape, not only by colour — red-on-black at low brightness is not a reliable sole channel.
  • The ring is always on. An alarm-only ring is a ring the driver has never learned to read, and the ring's steady presence is the only place sensor health can live. Solid = watching, dotted = blind or demo. An empty healthy ring is information.
  • The ring sits inside burnInScaffold and inherits pixel-shift; it adds no independent animation.

Known limitations, documented rather than fixed

By decision
  • Reversing. Art cars back up. In reverse the entire brake / rear logic inverts and there is no gear signal. Stated in the SurroundRing KDoc so it becomes a spotter-procedure item rather than a surprise.
  • Contact coasting. Thermal tracks drop out on occlusion. A person who vanishes at 3 m vanishes from the ring instantly rather than fading. Do not add tablet-side coasting until the Jetson's track-id churn is fixed — coasting by id while the producer churns ids draws one person as two.
  • The two views disagree about “how far left”. A forward contact's ring angle is its true bearing, but its figure x uses the 28° perspective magnification, so at az 20° the figure sits at 79 % screen width while the blip is 20° off vertical. Fixing that needs the perspective mapping redone; an ad-hoc fix is explicitly warned against.
  • A person walking parallel to the car at matched speed abeam is a textbook constant-bearing track and will flag. That belongs Jetson-side.
Outstanding — the acceptance gate

All phases of the surround HUD have landed and been verified against real bus traffic. But nobody has confirmed a #00421E rim and small hollow blips are legible at night brightness, through dust, by someone actually driving. One evening on-device at real brightness is the acceptance test. Until then this feature is built but not accepted.
