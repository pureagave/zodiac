:app · the cockpit

The three concepts

CockpitScreen dispatches on the active CockpitConcept. The active concept is picked via a top-right pill and persisted across launches; switching is purely presentational, because all three read the same StateFlow<CockpitUiState>.

RADAR — the M41A sweep scope

ui/concepts/MotionTrackerScreen. The Aliens M41A motion tracker: a circular scope whose sweep arm makes one revolution every 4 s and lights up the real Black Rock City map as it passes, with art and camp contacts pulsing behind it.

RADAR draws the map twice a frame — a dim base plus a lit re-blit clipped to the rotating sweep wedge — which is why the cached projected-map path exists at all, and why RADAR is the worst case for performance on the Fire HD 10.

MAP — the Nostromo instrument bay

ui/concepts/InstrumentBayScreen. The Alien Nostromo gauge wall: bordered tiles carrying a heading dial, a speed gauge, a ground-track map, and cell and throttle gauges.

Two tiles are literals

The cell gauges (CELL A 70%, CELL B 45%) and the throttle trace are hard-coded in the composable. They are not wired to anything, because the vehicle bus that would feed them does not exist.

What RADAR and MAP share

Both render the control strip (ConceptControls) and the operational readout (ui/ops/opsReadout) as a first-class themed footer, each in its own palette. The footer carries:

  • BRC clock and sunrise / sunset
  • Drive-to guidance — bearing, distance, and a heading-relative arrow to the active core/ops/NavTarget
  • A prominent full-width DRIVE TO bar (ui/ops/driveToBar) above the footer
  • Beacon health, flagged red at battery ≤ 20 % or fixQuality ≤ 0 or satellites < 5

Centre-viewport touch drives the map, not the vehicle. Drag to pan, pinch to zoom, two-finger twist to rotate (ui/playamap/MapTouchInput). Heading and speed are set programmatically or by the GPS fix, never by tapping the viewport. An earlier X→heading / Y→speed mapping was replaced by the map interaction.

DRIVER — the OLED night HUD

ui/concepts/DriverNightScreen. A mostly-black Star-Wars-vector display for the driver's phone. It is the one concept with a real safety job, and it is designed around a specific person:

Who this is for

A person driving a large, slow, loud art car through a crowd of pedestrians in the dark. Many are dressed dark, some are intoxicated, and the vehicle is loud enough that they may not hear it. The windscreen is the primary instrument. This display has exactly two jobs: the aft and blind arcs the driver's eyes cannot cover, and the machine-computed collision flag.

Every element must be absorbable in a sub-second glance or in peripheral vision. Anything that rewards sustained head-down study is a defect, not a feature.

The layout is a deliberate hybrid of two views:

ViewShowsJob
PerspectiveVanishing-point grid, hollow wireframe figures on the ground plane, size = proximity. Forward arc only (PERSPECTIVE_ARC_DEG = 30f).The detail view — where a driver judges one specific person.
Surround ringPlan view, nose-up, ego at centre, carries every contact including astern.The awareness view — “something is there”, not “here is what it looks like”.

Forward contacts appear twice, as a figure and as a blip. That redundancy is the price of keeping the figure work, and it is only legible because the ring marks which arc is duplicated below. The full HUD design →

The passenger toggle is hidden in both directions: a rider must not be able to leave passenger mode, and a driver's tablet must not fall into it.

Design history

The original pre-build concept exploration (Tactical Grid / CRT Vector / Dropship Console, written 2026-04) is kept in design/UI_CONCEPTS_ZODIAC.md but is superseded — none of the three was built as specified, and its palette (which included amber) is obsolete. Two later concepts, A CRT VECTOR and B PERSPECTIVE, were dropped on 2026-07-04 and the survivors lost their letter tags; DRIVER was added afterwards. It is kept because the mood boards and the reference-capture plan are still the source of the aesthetic.
